His argument was that philosophy must be applicable to everyday life, not confined to monasteries or intellectual debate.&#x20;

## **The move: from Trika metaphysics to a general theory of psychological transformation**

I'd build it in three layers.

LAYER 1 · PHILOSOPHICAL FOUNDATION

### **A theory of consciousness, identity and agency**

Derived from Abhinavagupta, Utpaladeva, Vivekananda and critical engagement with other traditions

LAYER 2 · PSYCHOLOGICAL SCIENCE

### **A general theory of suffering and transformation**

Explicit mechanisms, measurable variables, alternative explanations and falsifiable predictions

LAYER 3 · PRACTICAL APPLICATION

### **Therapy, education, relationships and AI**

Specific methods developed and evaluated for particular contexts

Notice that psychotherapy becomes just **one application of a more general framework**.

That matters. If the theory is truly general, it ought to illuminate learning, creativity, human relationships, identity formation, moral development and psychological suffering.

Not just what happens inside a counselling room.

## **What would the theory actually claim?**

Here's a first attempt, not a finished theory.

**Working name: Generative Theory of Agency and Self-Formation.**

| Principle          | General proposition                                                                                             |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Manifestation   | Human experience is actively structured through perception, interpretation, embodiment and action.              |
| 2. Differentiation | A functional sense of self depends on distinguishing oneself from other people and the environment.             |
| 3. Contraction     | Those distinctions can become rigid, restricting perceived possibilities for action.                            |
| 4. Suffering       | Some forms of psychological suffering arise when patterns of self-organisation become persistently maladaptive. |
| 5. Recognition     | Changes in how a person understands and experiences their identity may restore flexibility and agency.          |
| 6. Participation   | Relationships and environments influence which possibilities become available to a person.                      |

The distinctive part isn't claiming that thought influences experience. Psychology worked that out rather a long time ago.

The distinctive part is exploring **consciousness as intrinsically expressive, differentiation as an activity rather than simply an error, and therapeutic transformation as the reorganisation of agency**.

That is much closer to Abhinavagupta.

But the intellectual challenge is significant: these six propositions are not all deductions from his metaphysics, and some are already familiar within contemporary psychology. We'd need to show exactly what the new theory explains that existing theories cannot.

## **There's already a scientific framework to engage with**

[**Process-Based Therapy: A Personalized Approach to Treatment**](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/20438087231152848?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Moskow, Ong, Hayes, Hofmann and colleagues, 2023.

Process-based therapy is moving away from organising treatment entirely around diagnostic categories. Instead, it asks which psychological processes maintain a particular person's difficulties and which interventions might change them.&#x20;

You could develop a **distinctive philosophical and explanatory model within the wider study of psychological change**, then investigate whether it offers new predictions or treatment principles.

There is an even closer precedent.

## **How AI changes the equation**

This is where your Svatantrya infrastructure becomes more than a textual archive.

Imagine a system that can trace the entire chain of an idea:

**Sanskrit proposition**

A specific statement from Abhinavagupta, verified against the original text and commentarial context

**Formal argument**

What follows logically, which premises are added, and which objections remain

**Psychological model**

Definitions of mechanisms, causal hypotheses and competing explanations

**Empirical investigation**

Predictions, measurements, possible disconfirming results and ethical constraints

AI could help construct, challenge and revise that chain, including formal checks in Lean where logical formalisation is appropriate. It would not make metaphysical premises true or establish clinical effectiveness.

For example, the system might derive the hypothesis that increasing flexibility in self-boundaries improves emotional regulation in some circumstances.

Then the AI has to ask the uncomfortable questions: Does that prediction differ from existing treatments? What about people with dissociation? Can stable boundaries actually be protective? What evidence would disprove the proposal?

That's how you prevent it becoming a theory that explains absolutely everything and therefore predicts virtually nothing.

## **One disagreement: don't completely remove Trika**

I'd remove the **requirement to accept Trika**, not erase its intellectual origin or pretend its metaphysical premises are scientifically established.

There are three importantly different propositions:

1. **Metaphysical:** Consciousness is fundamental, intrinsically free and expressive.
2. **Psychological:** Some forms of suffering involve maladaptive restriction of perceived agency.
3. **Clinical:** Certain interventions can help people recover psychological flexibility.

You can argue for the first philosophically. You can investigate the second and third empirically. Evidence for the third doesn't automatically prove the first.

Keeping those distinctions visible makes the work intellectually stronger, not weaker.

It also respects a basic clinical principle: treatment must serve the client's interests, rather than requiring them to adopt the therapist's religious worldview.&#x20;
